Performance, part 2: Climb and field performance

This is the second part of Lecture 11, Performance Assessment. It covers the steady climb, the speeds for the best angle and the best rate, the takeoff speeds, the ground run and its integration, the balanced and critical field lengths, the landing, and the effect of altitude, temperature, and weight on the ground run. The equations of motion and the symbols are in Performance, part 1: Level flight, range, and endurance.

What to take from this lecture

  • The climb angle follows T−DT - DT−D and the rate of climb follows V (T−D)V\,(T - D)V(T−D). Compute both with the engine deck; the closed forms are a check.

  • The takeoff speeds follow from the stall speed, the control with an engine out, and the tail-strike angle, with the factors of MIL-STD-3013B.

  • Field lengths come from integrating the ground run at the altitude, the temperature, and the weight of the requirement.

The ground run¶

Along the runway, with the rolling friction on the weight the wheels carry:

WgdVdt=T−D−μ (W−L)−Wsin⁡θr\frac{W}{g}\frac{dV}{dt} = T - D - \mu\,(W - L) - W\sin\theta_rgW​dtdV​=T−D−μ(W−L)−Wsinθr​
(7)

  • LLL and DDD come from the takeoff polar in ground effect, at the angle of attack of the ground run.

  • TTT comes from the engine deck at the pressure altitude and the temperature of the runway. It varies along the run.

  • θr\theta_rθr​ is the runway slope, positive uphill. The equation is the form for a small slope.

  • Once L>WL > WL>W, the friction term is zero.

Balanced and critical field length¶

Definition 1 (Balanced field length)

The balanced field length is the runway length at which, after an engine failure at the engine-failure speed, the distance to continue the takeoff to the obstacle height equals the distance to stop. The critical field length of MIL-STD-3013B balances the distance to lift off against the distance to stop (Table 2 of Lecture 6).

An estimate for aircraft with two or more engines (Gudmundsson 2022, Eq. 18-33, after Torenbeek 1982), in feet:

Landing¶

In the braking roll:

WgdVdt=−[μB (W−L)+D−T]\frac{W}{g}\frac{dV}{dt} = -\left[\mu_B\,(W - L) + D - T\right]gW​dtdV​=−[μB​(W−L)+D−T]
(10)

  • TTT is the idle thrust, or negative with thrust reversers.

  • DDD comes from the landing polar, plus spoilers or speed brakes.

  • Spoilers dump the lift. The brakes need weight on the wheels.

  • The air distance and the flare are in Lecture 6. Integrate the braking roll as for the takeoff.

The source is Gudmundsson (2022), Eqs. 23-1 and 23-8 to 23-16.
